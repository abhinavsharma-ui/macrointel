1.1  Market Regime Detection
Edge
Correct regime labeling prevents applying trend logic during chop and mean-reversion logic during breakouts. Wrong regime = wrong system. Regime detection is the meta-layer all other rules depend on.
Formula/Rule
Regime = classify(ADX, ATR%, RV, OFI, VWAP slope, rolling Sharpe). Trend: ADX>25, price >VWAP, 5d return/ATR >1.5. Mean-Reversion: ADX<20, price repeatedly crossing VWAP, RV/HV ratio <0.8. Chop: ADX<15, close-to-close returns near 0, RV low. Vol Expansion: ATR% 1d >1.5× 20d avg, VIX or realized vol spike. Event Day: FOMC/CPI/earnings flag active, RV >2× baseline. Recommended: HMM (2–3 states) trained on rolling 60-day window retrained weekly. Alternative: XGBoost classifier on 6 features above, re-fit on expanding window.
Data Needed
OHLCV 1m+, ADX(14), ATR(14), rolling vol 5/20 days, VWAP intraday, economic calendar, earnings calendar, realized vol from options chain.
Best Markets
US equities (SPY, QQQ, individual stocks >1M shares ADV). Futures (ES, NQ, CL). Crypto (BTC-PERP, ETH-PERP).
Holding Time
Regime label refreshed every 15–30 min intraday; swing regime re-evaluated daily at close.
Stop/Target
Not a trade — this is a meta-rule. Wrong regime detection causes system-level losses. Validate with out-of-sample PnL by regime label.
Failure Modes
Regime flip lag — model confirms trend after it ends. Overfit regime on training data. FOMC/CPI not flagged as event day. Chop misclassified as trend when ADX marginally above 25.
Sources
Avellaneda & Lee (2010) "Statistical Arbitrage in the US Equities Market". Lopez de Prado "Advances in Financial ML" Ch. 2. Ernest Chan "Machine Trading" Ch. 7. Bookmap docs (regime via DOM pressure).
1.2  Execution Realism — Slippage, Fill Probability, Partial Fills
Edge
Most backtests overestimate returns by 30–60% by ignoring fill realism. Accurate execution modeling is the difference between a paper system and a live system.
Formula/Rule
Slippage model: E[slippage] = α × (order_size / ADV)^β × σ × sign(direction). Typical α=0.5, β=0.6 for US equities mid-cap. Fill probability for limit: P(fill) = 1 − exp(−λ × distance_to_mid / spread). Market impact: Almgren-Chriss model — TC = σ × η × (X/V) + γ × X²/V. Partial fill model: assume fills proportional to liquidity at each level. Crypto: bid-ask spread + taker fee + 0.5× last trade slippage. NSE: use NIFTY50 impact cost published by NSE.
Data Needed
Trade-by-trade timestamps, bid/ask at time of entry, ADV, spread history, broker fill confirmations. For crypto: L2 book snapshots at time of signal.
Best Markets
Apply everywhere. Worst slippage: small-cap stocks, alt-coins, illiquid options. Best fills: ES/NQ futures, BTC-PERP on Binance.
Holding Time
N/A — model applied at each order event.
Stop/Target
Add 1–3 bps per side to all backtested trades as floor. Reject setups where expected edge < 2× estimated slippage.
Failure Modes
Assuming mid-price fills. Ignoring spread on limit orders. Not modeling cancel latency. Crypto: ignoring funding rate paid while waiting for fill. NSE: ignoring STT on sell-side.
Sources
Almgren & Chriss "Optimal Execution of Portfolio Transactions" (2000). Kissell & Glantz "Optimal Trading Strategies". Hendershott, Jones, Menkveld (2011) JF. NSE impact cost: nseindia.com/products/content/equities/equities/impact_cost.htm
1.3  Risk Sizing — Per-Setup Expectancy, Stop Distance, Kill Switches
Edge
Correct position sizing (Kelly/fractional Kelly) compounds returns optimally. Oversizing during low-expectancy periods is the #1 cause of ruin.
Formula/Rule
Kelly fraction: f* = (bp − q) / b where b = avg_win/avg_loss, p = win rate, q = 1−p. Use 25% Kelly to start (quarter Kelly). Per-trade risk: R$ = account × risk_pct (e.g., 0.5–1%). Shares = R$ / (entry − stop). Expectancy = (win_rate × avg_win) − (loss_rate × avg_loss). Only trade if expectancy > 2× slippage. Time stop: exit if trade not moving in direction within N bars (setup-specific). Daily Kill Switch: halt trading if daily_loss > 2% account OR 3 consecutive losers in same setup.
Data Needed
Per-setup win rate, avg win/loss from walk-forward, current account equity, ATR for stop distance, daily PnL tracker.
Best Markets
Universal. Fractional Kelly is critical in crypto due to volatility. ES/NQ: tighter risk pct acceptable due to tight bid-ask.
Holding Time
Recalculate Kelly monthly using rolling 3-month stats. Daily kill switch is session-level.
Stop/Target
Stop = 1–1.5× ATR below entry (swing), 0.25–0.5× ATR (day trade). Target = 2–3R minimum. Time stop = 2 bars (scalp) to 2 days (swing).
Failure Modes
Full Kelly during drawdown. Fixed share size ignoring volatility. No kill switch after bad fills. Using theoretical expectancy not walk-forward expectancy.
Sources
Tharp "Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom". Kelly (1956). Van Tharp Institute (vantharp.com). Pardo "The Evaluation and Optimization of Trading Strategies".
1.4  Intraday Microstructure — OFI, Microprice, Queue Pressure, Sweeps
Edge
Short-term price direction (next 5–30s) is predictable with 53–58% accuracy using order-flow imbalance and microprice. This is the backbone of scalping and tight entry timing for day trades.
Formula/Rule
OFI = Σ(bid_size_change − ask_size_change) over window T. Microprice = (bid×ask_qty + ask×bid_qty) / (bid_qty + ask_qty). Queue pressure = bid_depth[0:3] / ask_depth[0:3] (top 3 levels). Sweep detection: trade size > 3× avg trade, consumes multiple levels, price moves >0.5× spread in 500ms. Signed aggressor flow = sum of taker buy volume − taker sell volume per minute. Use OFI as real-time signal: if OFI >+1σ → lean long next 30s. Mean-revert OFI for countertrend, follow OFI for trend regime.
Data Needed
L2 order book (top 10 levels min) at 100ms or better granularity, timestamped trades with aggressor side flag, L3 for queue position (optional).
Best Markets
ES/NQ futures, BTC-PERP/ETH-PERP on Binance. US large-cap equities with L2 (AAPL, TSLA, NVDA, SPY). NSE NIFTY50 stocks (NSE order book via NSE APIs).
Holding Time
5 seconds to 5 minutes depending on signal strength.
Stop/Target
Tight: 1–2 ticks stop, 2–4 ticks target. Exit on OFI reversal or microprice crossing entry mid.
Failure Modes
Stale L2 data (>200ms). Spoofed orders inflating OFI. OFI fails in illiquid books. Not adjusting for spread regime (wide spread = OFI less reliable).
Sources
Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (2014) "The Price Impact of Order Book Events". Stoikov (2018) "The Micro-Price: A High Frequency Estimator of Future Prices". Cao, Chen & Griffin (2005). tardis.dev microstructure notebooks.
1.5  Validation — Walk-Forward Testing, Transaction Cost, Paper-vs-Live Drift
Edge
In-sample optimization is curve fitting. Only walk-forward (or combinatorial purged cross-validation) gives honest estimate of live performance. Adding TC modeling converts paper alpha to live alpha.
Formula/Rule
Walk-forward protocol: train on T months, test on 1 month, slide forward. Minimum 12 test folds. Purged k-fold CV: add embargo period = holding_time × 2 between train and test folds to prevent leakage. TC-adjusted return = gross_return − (2 × half_spread + slippage + commission) × turnover. Paper-vs-live drift score = (live_sharpe / paper_sharpe). Acceptable if >0.6. Overfitting score: IS_Sharpe / OOS_Sharpe ratio. If >2× → overfit. Reduce parameters.
Data Needed
Full tick or 1-min OHLCV history >3 years. Trade execution logs (for paper-vs-live comparison). Commission schedule. Slippage from live fills.
Best Markets
US equities: Databento Nasdaq TotalView-ITCH. Crypto: Tardis.dev full book replay. NSE: NSE EOD + Truedata tick.
Holding Time
N/A — validation framework. Run before deploying any system.
Stop/Target
System passes validation if: OOS Sharpe >0.8, max drawdown <2× expected from IS, win rate within 5% of IS.
Failure Modes
Look-ahead bias (using future data). Survivorship bias (only backtesting stocks that still exist). Ignoring corporate actions. Insufficient test folds. Ignoring slippage on large positions.
Sources
Lopez de Prado "Advances in Financial ML" (Ch 7–12). Pardo "Evaluation & Optimization of Trading Strategies". Bailey et al (2016) "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting". Quantopian lectures (quantopian.com/lectures).
1.6  PnL Attribution — Setup, Regime, Symbol, Time Window
Edge
Without attribution, you cannot know which setups are alive and which are dead. Most systems have 1–2 setups doing all the work and several dragging returns negative.
Formula/Rule
Tag every trade at entry with: setup_id, regime, symbol, session (open/mid/close), direction. Aggregate daily: PnL_by_setup[s] = Σ trade_pnl where trade.setup == s. Sortino by setup = avg_return / downside_std. Profit factor = gross_wins / gross_losses per setup. Cull setups with PF <1.2 over 100 trades. Time-of-day attribution: bin trades by 30-min bucket, plot PnL curve. Kill underperforming time windows.
Data Needed
Tagged trade log (setup, regime, symbol, entry/exit time, PnL). Python/pandas for aggregation. Dashboard for visualization.
Best Markets
Universal — run attribution across all three lanes simultaneously.
Holding Time
Rerun attribution weekly (intraday), monthly (swing).
Stop/Target
Retire a setup if: PF <1.0 over 50 trades, or drawdown >3× expected. Reduce size if Sortino drops below 0.5.
Failure Modes
Not tagging trades at entry. Mixing multiple setups in one P&L view. Confirmation bias — rationalizing bad setups. Not separating commissions from gross PnL.
Sources
Kestner "Quantitative Trading Strategies". Covel "Trend Following". Risk.net — execution analytics primer.
1.7  Portfolio Construction — Correlation Caps, Capital Split Across 3 Lanes
Edge
Uncorrelated strategies compound returns with lower drawdown (Shannon entropy of returns). Mixing uncorrelated lanes reduces max drawdown vs single-strategy deployment.
Formula/Rule
Target Sharpe of combined system > individual max. Correlation cap: no two concurrent positions >0.6 corr on rolling 20-day. Capital allocation starting point: Swing 40% / Day Trading 35% / Crypto Scalper 25%. Adjust monthly using RCTR (risk-contribution targeting): each lane contributes equal risk. Risk parity: w_i = (1/σ_i) / Σ(1/σ_j). Sector cap: no >25% of equity capital in same sector. Max single name: 5% of capital.
Data Needed
Daily returns by lane, correlation matrix (rolling 20-day), realized vol per lane, sector/industry tags.
Best Markets
Swing: US equities + ETFs. Day: US equities + ES/NQ futures. Crypto: BTC/ETH perps.
Holding Time
Allocation rebalanced monthly or after >15% drawdown in any lane.
Stop/Target
Lane kill switch: if any lane hits −3% in one week, halve allocation. Restore after 10 winning trading days.
Failure Modes
Treating correlated setups as uncorrelated (e.g., AAPL breakout + QQQ breakout = same risk). Over-allocating to hot lane after good run. Ignoring vol regime shift (crypto vol 5× equities vol).
Sources
Markowitz (1952). Qian (2005) "Risk Parity Portfolios". AQR white papers (aqr.com/insights). Ilmanen "Expected Returns".
1.8  News/Event Edge — Filings, Earnings, Exchange Announcements, Macro Releases
Edge
Scheduled events (earnings, FOMC, CPI) create vol expansion and directional momentum that can be timed. Surprise magnitude correlates strongly with post-event drift duration.
Formula/Rule
Earnings surprise score = (actual_EPS − consensus_EPS) / σ(historical_surprise). Post-earnings drift (PED): buy if surprise >+1σ, hold 2–5 days. Average 4–6% move for large beats. FOMC: go flat 30 min before, re-enter after initial 5-min spike absorbs. Direction follow-through: 70% in first hour, then 50/50. CPI: trade the implied vol expansion pre-event (straddle), then fade 30-min post-spike if within range. Exchange announcements: circuit breaker reopens, trading halts resumptions → high win rate fade of opening spike.
Data Needed
Earnings calendar (Refinitiv, Bloomberg, EarningsWhispers), FOMC/CPI schedule (Federal Reserve, BLS), 8-K/10-Q filings (SEC EDGAR), news timestamped feed (Benzinga, Dow Jones Newswires).
Best Markets
US equities earnings: SPY members, high beta stocks. Macro: ES/NQ/DXY/TLT. Crypto: BTC around CPI/FOMC highly correlated post-2023.
Holding Time
2–5 days (earnings drift). 30 min–2 hours (FOMC/CPI intraday play). 1–3 days (filing continuation).
Stop/Target
Stop below low of event-day candle. Target = 1.5× event day range. For drift: stop on close below 5-day MA.
Failure Modes
Buying after move fully priced in. Holding through second earnings (guidance miss). CPI straddle not accounting for IV crush. Earnings in wrong direction despite beat (sell-the-news).
Sources
Jegadeesh & Titman (1993). Bernard & Thomas (1989) post-earnings drift. SEC EDGAR (sec.gov/edgar). EarningsWhispers.com. Refinitiv earnings calendar API.
1.9  Adaptive Exits — Trailing Logic, Regime-Flip Exits, Signal-Fade Exits
Edge
Fixed targets leave money on the table in trending markets. Dynamic exits extract more from winners while protecting against reversals.
Formula/Rule
ATR trailing stop: stop = high_since_entry − 2× ATR(14). Update every bar. Regime-flip exit: if regime changes from Trend → Chop or MeanRev → exit immediately regardless of PnL. Signal-fade exit: if entry signal (e.g., OFI) reverses >50% of initial reading → exit. Parabolic SAR trailing: AF starts 0.02, increases by 0.02 per new high, max 0.2. Volatility-scaled target: target = entry + N × ATR where N = regime_multiplier (2 in trend, 1.2 in chop). Time-based exit: if >T bars without reaching 0.5× target → exit at market.
Data Needed
Real-time ATR, live regime indicator, OFI or original entry signal in real-time, position tracking system.
Best Markets
Universal. Most impactful for day trading and scalping where moves are brief.
Holding Time
Exit rules trigger within seconds (scalp) to days (swing).
Stop/Target
Initial stop set at entry. Trail via ATR or SAR. Signal-fade or regime-flip creates immediate hard exit.
Failure Modes
Trailing stop too tight (stopped out before target). Regime flip lag causes late exit. Signal-fade logic reverses mid-move unnecessarily. ATR not recalculated during high-volatility sessions.
Sources
Wilder "New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems" (SAR). Chan "Algorithmic Trading" (dynamic exits). Kaufman "Trading Systems and Methods" 5th ed.
1.10  Time-of-Day Edge — Open, Midday, Power Hour, Overnight
Edge
Intraday PnL distribution is highly non-uniform. First 30 min and last 45 min (power hour) account for ~65% of daily intraday opportunity. Midday is structurally low-edge for most setups.
Formula/Rule
US Equities: 9:30–10:00 = highest vol, widest spreads, best ORB/sweep setups. 10:00–11:30 = continuation + VWAP setups. 11:30–14:00 = chop zone, reduce size 50%. 14:00–15:00 = momentum resumption. 15:00–16:00 = power hour, highest institutional flow, best mean-reversion and momentum. After-hours: avoid except for earnings plays (thin book). Overnight gap: gap >1% with news → hold through open with tight stop. Gap without news → fade at first 5-min rejection. Crypto: Asia session (00:00–08:00 UTC) = lower liquidity, funding-driven moves. Europe (07:00–13:00 UTC) = trend initiation. US (13:00–21:00 UTC) = highest volume, follow equities sentiment.
Data Needed
Intraday OHLCV by 30-min bucket over 2+ years, trade-tagged with session timestamps, overnight gap tracker.
Best Markets
US equities (SPY/QQQ/individual). ES/NQ futures (pre-market 8:30 EST most important). Crypto 24/7 but sessions matter.
Holding Time
Session-specific: 5 min–90 min for open and power hour. Overnight: hold to next open or first 30 min.
Stop/Target
Open plays: stop = 5-min low (long) or 5-min high (short). Power hour: stop = VWAP. Overnight gap fade: stop above gap high.
Failure Modes
Trading midday with same size as open. Ignoring volume confirmation (low-volume breakout at open = trap). Not adjusting for half-day sessions or market holidays.
Sources
Admati & Pfleiderer (1988) "A Theory of Intraday Patterns". Wood, McInish & Ord (1985). SteadyOptions — time of day research. Quantpedia.com — intraday anomalies.

2.1  Earnings Propagation & Peer Spillover
Edge
When a sector leader reports strong earnings, peers move in sympathy within 1–3 days even before their own earnings. This propagation is most reliable in semi-conductor, banking, and retail sectors.
Formula/Rule
Identify sector leader report. If EPS surprise >+1σ: scan peers for stocks NOT yet reported. Rank peers by: (1) same sub-industry, (2) revenue correlation >0.6, (3) similar PE multiple. Enter peer within 2 days of leader report, before peer own earnings. Exit before peer reports (avoid binary event). Size = 50% of normal R due to binary risk of peer own report.
Data Needed
Earnings calendar, sector classification (GICS), revenue correlation matrix, surprise magnitude API (Refinitiv/Bloomberg).
Best Markets
US equities: semis (NVDA → AMD), banks (JPM → BAC, C), retail (WMT → TGT, COST).
Holding Time
1–4 days. Exit before peer own earnings if still open.
Stop/Target
Stop = close below entry candle low. Target = 1.5× average peer-day-1 move from historical data.
Failure Modes
Holding through peer own earnings. Wrong sector correlation (different product lines). Macro overrides sector (risk-off day kills all longs regardless of earnings).
Sources
Ramnath (2002) "Underreaction to Competitor Earnings". Firth (1976). Quantpedia strategy #185. Seeking Alpha earnings propagation studies.
2.2  Official Filings & Exchange Announcement Continuation
Edge
8-K filings with material news (CEO change, acquisition, guidance raise) cause 2–8% moves in first 2 hours. Stocks continue drifting in filing direction 60–70% of time through day 3.
Formula/Rule
Monitor SEC EDGAR 8-K RSS in real-time (free). Classify filing: accretive (buyback, upgrade, deal) vs dilutive (offering, write-down). If accretive + first hour momentum confirms → enter long. Circuit breaker/halt resume: price first moves in news direction ~65% of time. Trade direction of move within first 3 minutes after resume. Stop = below pre-halt price for longs.
Data Needed
SEC EDGAR full-text search API (efts.sec.gov), real-time news feed (Benzinga, Dow Jones), halt/resume feed from exchange.
Best Markets
US equities, mid/large cap. NIFTY50 stocks for NSE exchange announcements.
Holding Time
1–3 days for continuation drift.
Stop/Target
Stop = below day-of-filing low. Target = 2× day-1 move.
Failure Modes
Acting on unclassified 8-K (routine updates have no edge). Filing after market close → gap opens fully priced. Not checking if news was already leaked pre-market.
Sources
SEC EDGAR (sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar). Tetlock, Saar-Tsechansky, Macskassy (2008). Benzinga Pro API.
2.3  Multi-Day News Drift & Sentiment Decay
Edge
After major news, institutional accumulation/distribution takes 3–10 days to fully express. NLP-based sentiment on news articles predicts 1–5 day drift direction with 54–58% accuracy.
Formula/Rule
Sentiment score S = NLP_pos_score − NLP_neg_score on headline + body, normalized −1 to +1. Entry: if S >+0.4 on day of news and price >5d MA → long. Decay model: expected drift = S × β × exp(−λ × days_since_event). β≈0.015/day, λ≈0.25. Exit when: (1) sentiment decays below 0.2, (2) follow-up news contradicts, (3) 7 trading days elapsed.
Data Needed
News NLP feed (Refinitiv Sentiment, Bloomberg News Analytics, RavenPack, free alternative: VADER on Benzinga RSS), daily OHLCV.
Best Markets
US large-cap equities, sector ETFs.
Holding Time
3–7 trading days.
Stop/Target
Stop = close below 8-day EMA. Target = 1.5–2× entry day range.
Failure Modes
Counter-narrative news killing drift early. Sentiment model trained on different news types. Market regime flip (risk-off) overrides individual stock drift.
Sources
Tetlock (2007) "Giving Content to Investor Sentiment". RavenPack (ravenpack.com/research). FinBERT model (GitHub: ProsusAI/finbert). Loughran-McDonald financial sentiment dictionary.
2.4  Gap Continuation vs Gap Fade
Edge
Gaps >0.5% with volume confirmation and news catalyst continue 65% of time. Gaps without news and with low relative volume fade to fill 70% of time within 3 days.
Formula/Rule
Gap % = (open − prev_close) / prev_close. Continue if: gap% >1%, relative volume >1.5× avg, news catalyst confirmed, opens above/below prior day range. Fade if: gap% <0.5%, no news, relative volume <1.2×, opens inside prior day range. Continuation entry: buy first 5-min pullback to VWAP or open after 9:35 (allow price discovery). Fade entry: sell first bounce to prior close or gap fill 50% level.
Data Needed
Pre-market data (4:00–9:30), overnight news, relative volume at open vs 20-day avg, prior day OHLCV.
Best Markets
US large and mid-cap equities. Works well on ETFs (QQQ, IWM).
Holding Time
Gap fade: intraday (close before EOD). Gap continuation: 1–3 days.
Stop/Target
Continuation: stop = below open. Target = prior resistance. Fade: stop = above gap high. Target = gap fill (prior close).
Failure Modes
Gap continuation failing on macro down day. Fade setup getting squeezed by second wave of buying. Half-day or thin pre-market creating false gap readings.
Sources
Bhattacharya (2005) gap study. Bulkowski "Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns". StockAnalysis.com gap stats. SMB Capital gap methodology.
2.5  Sector Rotation & Factor Rotation
Edge
Capital flows between sectors in predictable patterns tied to economic cycle phase. Identifying rotation 2–5 days early allows entry before main move.
Formula/Rule
Relative strength: RS_sector = (sector_return − SPY_return) over 5/20 days. Rotation signal: buy sectors with RS improving over last 5 days while broad market flat/down. Factor rotation: growth→value rotation when 10Y yield rising; value→growth when yield falling. Quantitative rotation: rank 11 SPDR sector ETFs by 1-month RS, buy top 3, sell bottom 3. Rebalance monthly. Leading indicators: Financials lead markets up, Utilities lead markets down (defensive rotation).
Data Needed
Sector ETF daily prices (XLF, XLK, XLE, XLV, etc.), SPY, factor data (Fama-French from Ken French website, free).
Best Markets
SPDR sector ETFs, sector-specific stocks. NSE sectoral indices for Indian market.
Holding Time
1–4 weeks for rotation plays.
Stop/Target
Stop = sector ETF closes below 20-day MA. Target = RS reaching top decile.
Failure Modes
Mistaking noise for rotation. Macro shock interrupts rotation. Sector with geopolitical risk despite strong RS. Factor crowding causing sudden reversal.
Sources
SPDR Sector ETF data (ssga.com). Ken French data library (mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french). Fidelity sector rotation model.
2.6  Macro Driver Sensitivity by Sector
Edge
Different sectors have different correlations to macro factors (rates, oil, dollar, credit spreads). This allows macro-driven sector selection before the move completes.
Formula/Rule
Regression: sector_return = α + β_rates×ΔTY10 + β_dollar×ΔDXY + β_oil×ΔCL + ε. Known sensitivities: Financials (XLF) +0.8 corr to rate rises. Energy (XLE) +0.9 to oil. Tech (XLK) −0.7 to rate rises. Utilities (XLU) −0.6 to rate rises. Trade: when 10Y breaks above key level, rotate into XLF/XLE, reduce XLK/XLU. Update betas quarterly using rolling 60-day regression.
Data Needed
Daily prices: UST 10Y yield (FRED), DXY, CL1 (crude oil), sector ETFs. FRED API is free.
Best Markets
US sector ETFs. Futures: ZN (rates), DX (dollar), CL (oil) as hedges.
Holding Time
1–3 weeks (macro trend).
Stop/Target
Stop = macro factor reverts beyond 1.5σ from entry. Target = beta-implied move.
Failure Modes
Regime shift changes historical betas. Macro factor moves but transmission to sector delayed (e.g., oil spike not reflected in XLE for days). Correlation breakdown during crisis.
Sources
FRED (fred.stlouisfed.org). CME macro factor models. Deutsche Bank macro sensitivity research. Investopedia sector sensitivity primer.
2.7  Options Flow & Implied Volatility Information
Edge
Large unusual options activity (UOA) by smart money predicts directional moves in underlying 55–62% of time within 5 days. IV skew reveals market expectation of direction.
Formula/Rule
UOA signal: call or put volume >3× 20-day avg open interest, out of money, expiry 2–6 weeks out. IV Skew: skew = IV(25Δ put) − IV(25Δ call). Extreme put skew → near-term bearish bet by institutions. Put/Call ratio: PCR >1.2 on individual stock → contrarian bullish (fear peak). PCR <0.5 → contrarian bearish. IV crush trade: sell straddle after earnings confirmation (after announcement, not before). GEX (Gamma Exposure): aggregate dealer gamma. Negative GEX → dealers amplify moves. Positive GEX → dealers dampen moves.
Data Needed
Options chain L1 data (Tradier free API, TD Ameritrade, Interactive Brokers). Unusual options activity feed (Cheddar Flow, Market Chameleon, Unusual Whales — $30–$50/mo).
Best Markets
US equities with active options (AAPL, TSLA, SPY, QQQ, NVDA, AMC-type high-IV names).
Holding Time
2–5 days (following smart money options flow).
Stop/Target
Stop = position goes against flow direction by >1% (suggests institutional exit). Target = 2× entry day move or 1-week expiry of referenced option.
Failure Modes
Hedging flow misidentified as directional (dealer hedging ≠ speculative bet). IV crush destroys long options even when right on direction. Dark pool prints not captured in options data.
Sources
Unusual Whales (unusualwhales.com). Market Chameleon (marketchameleon.com). SpotGamma GEX data. CBOE Skew Index. Hu (2014) "Does the Option Market React to Earnings Surprise?"
2.8  Cross-Asset Signals — Rates, Dollar, Oil, Index Futures
Edge
Cross-asset correlations create lead-lag relationships. ES/NQ futures move 4–6 hours before European cash open, providing pre-market signals. Dollar-denominated assets (gold, EM, oil) have predictable correlation structure.
Formula/Rule
ES/NQ overnight futures → predict SPY/QQQ open direction. If ES futures +0.5% or more overnight → 70% prob SPY opens green. DXY inverse relationship: when DXY breaks down → EM stocks/gold outperform (correlation -0.7 historically). Oil-Energy lead: XLE typically moves with CL futures 1:1 in vol, with 30-60 min lag. TLT-Rate play: falling TLT (rising rates) → rotate to XLF, reduce XLU/XLRE. VIX lead: VIX >20 → reduce position sizes. VIX >30 → defensive only. VIX <15 → full risk-on.
Data Needed
ES/NQ continuous futures (CME data), DXY index, CL futures, TLT daily, VIX (CBOE). All available free via Yahoo Finance for EOD or paid via IQFeed/Quandl for intraday.
Best Markets
US equities via cross-asset lead. ES/NQ for futures signals. EM ETFs (EEM) via DXY.
Holding Time
1 day (overnight futures signal) to 2 weeks (macro cross-asset trend).
Stop/Target
Use cross-asset confirmation only as filter, not primary signal. Stop = cross-asset signal reverses (DXY recovers, VIX spikes).
Failure Modes
Correlation breaks during crisis (flight-to-safety dominates everything). Overnight futures move reversed at open (buy the rumor, sell the news). Treating correlation as causation.
Sources
Bloomberg cross-asset desk research. Ilmanen "Expected Returns" Ch. 14. CME Institute macro primer. Yahoo Finance free cross-asset data.
2.9  Leader-Laggard Behavior Inside Sectors
Edge
Within each sector, 1–2 liquid "leaders" move first (in price and volume) before laggards follow. Trading laggards after leaders break out yields 1–3 day follow-through with reduced binary risk.
Formula/Rule
Leader identification: highest ADV + highest options open interest + most analyst coverage in sector. Leader-laggard lag ≈ 1–4 hours for intraday, 1–2 days for swing. Signal: leader breaks above 20-day high with volume >1.5× → buy sector laggards (same GICS sub-industry) within 2 hours. Correlation threshold: enter laggard only if 20-day rolling corr to leader >0.55.
Data Needed
Daily price + volume for sector members, GICS classifications, rolling 20-day correlation matrix (pandas + yfinance or Polygon.io).
Best Markets
Semiconductors (NVDA leads, AMD/SMCI/AMAT lag). Banks (JPM leads, BAC/WFC lag). Oil services (SLB leads). NSE IT sector (TCS leads, Infosys/Wipro lag).
Holding Time
1–3 days.
Stop/Target
Stop = laggard closes below prior day low. Target = laggard reaches leader's % move.
Failure Modes
Leader reversal before laggard moves. Correlation breaks (company-specific news on laggard). Sector rotation mid-trade.
Sources
Lo & MacKinlay (1990) "When Are Contrarian Profits Due to Stock Market Overreaction?" Hou (2007) "Industry Information Diffusion". Quantpedia lead-lag strategies.

Close in bottom 20% of range → next day opens red 64% of time. Follow-through filter: confirm with next-day pre-market futures direction before entering.

SECTION 3
Day Trading System — Intraday Edge & Setups
3.1  Opening Range Breakout (ORB) & Retest Quality
Edge
The first 5–30 minutes of a session establishes a high/low range. Breakout of this range with volume confirmation continues intraday 58–65% of time. Retest quality (tight retest = stronger) improves win rate to 68%.
Formula/Rule
Opening range: define as high/low of first N minutes (5, 15, or 30 min). Test all three. Entry trigger: price closes above ORB high on 1-min bar with volume >1.5× avg bar volume at that time. Retest quality: if price pulls back to ORB level within 15 min and holds on 3 consecutive closes → higher probability. Filter: relative volume at open must be >1.3×. Gap direction must match breakout direction.
Data Needed
Real-time 1-min OHLCV, relative volume tracker, pre-market high/low, L2 at breakout level.
Best Markets
US equities with RV >1.5× (stocks-in-play). ES/NQ 5-min ORB. NSE NIFTY50 large caps 9:15–9:45 range.
Holding Time
30 min–3 hours. Exit by 14:00 if not at target.
Stop/Target
Stop = below ORB low (long) or above ORB high (short). Target = 2× ORB height projected from breakout.
Failure Modes
ORB breakout in low-volume stock (fakeout). Chop regime → breakout reverses immediately. News after open invalidates pre-market analysis. Too tight an ORB (noise breaks it repeatedly).
Sources
Toby Crabel "Day Trading with Short-Term Price Patterns". SMB Capital ORB methodology. Quantpedia ORB strategy analysis. Tradingview ORB Pine Script (public).
3.2  VWAP Pullback Continuation
Edge
After establishing a trend in the first 90 min, stocks with high relative volume frequently use VWAP as dynamic support/resistance. First pullback to VWAP in trend direction has 60–65% win rate.
Formula/Rule
VWAP = Σ(typical_price × volume) / Σ(volume) where typical_price = (H+L+C)/3. Anchored VWAP (from open): recalculated each bar. Setup: stock trending above VWAP (in uptrend), pulls back to within 0.1% of VWAP, L2 shows bid support forming. Entry: buy as price bounces off VWAP with volume confirmation and bullish 1-min close. Standard Deviation bands: VWAP ± 1σ and ± 2σ. Price at VWAP+2σ = overbought, fade. Price at VWAP−1σ after uptrend = pullback entry.
Data Needed
Real-time 1-min OHLCV with volume (IQFeed, Polygon.io, or direct broker feed). L2 for entry timing.
Best Markets
US equities stocks-in-play (high RV). ES/NQ futures using cumulative day's VWAP. Crypto (BTC, ETH) using session VWAP.
Holding Time
15–90 minutes.
Stop/Target
Stop = 1× ATR below VWAP (or 2 closes below). Target = prior day's high or VWAP + 2σ.
Failure Modes
3rd or 4th VWAP test = weaker. Using VWAP in chop regime (no clear trend). Stocks too far from VWAP have no magnetic effect. Low-volume pullback = no institutional interest.
Sources
Brian Shannon "Anchored VWAP" (anchoredvwap.com). SMB Capital VWAP guide. Tradingview VWAP indicator. Kroll "A Complete Guide to Technical Trading Tactics".
3.3  Liquidity Sweep Reversal
Edge
Market makers and algorithms trigger stop clusters below/above obvious technical levels to generate liquidity before reversing. Identifying sweep (price moves through level, instantly reclaims) provides 60–70% win rate reversal entry.
Formula/Rule
Sweep detection: price moves beyond prior day low/high or round number by 0.1–0.3%, volume spikes >2× avg for that 1-min bar, then price closes back inside level in same candle. Entry: on candle following sweep-reclaim, in direction of reclaim. OFI confirmation: sweep should show taker sell spike (for low sweep) followed by immediate OFI flip to positive. L2 confirmation: bid stack rebuilds quickly above swept level.
Data Needed
Real-time 1-min OHLCV, volume, L2 order book, prior day high/low, round-number levels, cumulative delta.
Best Markets
ES/NQ futures (highest reliability — well-known stop clusters). US large-cap equities. BTC-PERP (frequent stop sweeps on Binance round numbers).
Holding Time
5–45 minutes.
Stop/Target
Stop = below sweep extreme. Target = VWAP or prior day mid.
Failure Modes
Genuine breakdown disguised as sweep (price doesn't reclaim). Sweep in direction of larger trend (macro momentum overrides reversal). Slow L2 data missing the speed of sweep.
Sources
Bookmap.com — liquidity sweep identification. ICT (Inner Circle Trader) liquidity concepts. SMB Capital stop hunt methodology. Jigsaw Trading order flow training.
3.4  Relative Volume & Volatility Expansion
Edge
High relative volume (RV >2×) stocks have 2–3× normal range. Trading high-RV names in direction of first 15-min trend produces statistically better outcomes than random stock selection.
Formula/Rule
RV = today_volume_so_far / avg_volume_at_same_time_of_day (20-day avg). Use pre-market RV at 9:15 for morning watch list. Vol expansion: ATR(today) > 1.5× ATR(20-day). Stock-in-play (SIP) criteria: RV >2×, news catalyst present, float <50M preferred. Filter out: RV >5× on no news (potential manipulation). RV >2× but spread >0.5% (hard to trade).

Pre-market volume data (9:00–9:30), intraday volume, 20-day avg volume by time-of-day, news feed.
Best Markets
US equities, small and mid-cap. Pre-market scanners: Trade Ideas, Finviz, Benzinga, thinkorswim scanner.
Holding Time
Intraday only.
Stop/Target
Defined by setup type (ORB, VWAP, sweep). RV is a filter, not a standalone setup.
Failure Modes
High RV with no news (thin float pump). RV calculated incorrectly (off-peak days). Using total day volume vs time-normalized volume.
Sources
Trade Ideas scanner docs. SMB Capital stock-in-play criteria. Warrior Trading watchlist methodology. Benzinga pre-market scanner.
3.5  Intraday Regime Switching Rules
Edge
A trending open (9:30–11:00) often transitions to a choppy midday (11:00–13:30). Strategies must switch between trend-following and mean-reversion modes to maintain positive expectancy through the session.
Formula/Rule
Regime indicator: 5-min ADX. ADX >25 = trend mode. ADX <20 = chop mode. Trend mode rules: VWAP pullbacks, ORB continuation, momentum entries. Chop mode rules: fade extremes, target VWAP as mean, tighten stops 50%, reduce size 50%. Switch signal: if 3 consecutive failed trend setups → switch to chop mode. Power hour trigger (14:30 onward): re-enter trend mode if ADX rises again or if price breaks midday range.
Data Needed
Real-time 5-min ADX, session win/loss tracker (failed setup counter), VWAP, midday range high/low.
Best Markets
US equities. ES/NQ futures (explicit midday regime is well-documented).
Holding Time
N/A — mode switching framework.
Stop/Target
Chop mode: tighten stops, 1:1 R/R only. Trend mode: 2:1+ R/R, trail stops.
Failure Modes
Not switching fast enough (3 consecutive losses signal needed sooner). Switching too early (1 failed trade not enough). ADX lag in volatile environments.
Sources
Wilder "New Concepts" (ADX). SMB Capital intraday regime framework. TastyTrade midday trading research. Davey "Building Winning Algorithmic Trading Systems".
3.6  Cumulative Delta & Trade-Flow Proxies
Edge
Cumulative delta (CD) = running sum of (buy volume − sell volume). Divergence between CD and price is a leading indicator of reversal. CD alignment with price = trend confirmation.
Formula/Rule
CD = Σ(aggressor_buy_volume − aggressor_sell_volume) since session open. Classify trade as buy if: price = ask or higher. Sell if: price = bid or lower. Mid-price = split. Divergence signal: price makes new high but CD fails to make new high → distribution (bearish). Price makes new low but CD fails → absorption (bullish). Cumulative delta reset: reset at session open or after large gap.
Data Needed
Tick-by-tick trades with aggressor side flag. Requires L3 or classified trades. Available on: ES/NQ via CQG/Rithmic, stocks via Bookmap, crypto via Tardis.dev.
Best Markets
ES/NQ futures (most robust CD signal). BTC-PERP/ETH-PERP. Large-cap US equities with active tape.
Holding Time
Signal-based: 5–60 minutes after divergence detected.
Stop/Target
Divergence trade: stop = new extreme beyond divergence high/low. Target = CD mean reversion to price alignment.
Failure Calls
Iceberg orders invalidating CD (hidden supply absorbed). HFT spoofing inflating buy side. Low-liquidity periods where CD is noisy.
Sources
Jigsaw Trading cumulative delta guide (jigsawtrading.com). Bookmap order flow education. Sierra Chart CD indicator documentation. Dalton "Mind Over Markets".

3.9  Failed Breakout / Failed Breakdown Setups
Edge
Failed breakouts occur when price breaks a key level with momentum, then reverses back within 2–3 bars. These setups have 62–68% win rate because trapped longs provide fuel for the reversal.

Failed breakout: price breaks above 20-day high or prior resistance, makes 2+ closes above, then closes back below. Enter short on reclose below level. Volume signature: breakout bar has high volume, but follow-through bar has declining volume (no conviction). Failed breakdown (bull trap): same in reverse. Price breaks below support, immediately reclaims. Enter long. Trap confirmation: L2 shows asks rebuilding below breakout level (for long trap) immediately after close-back.

Stop/Target

Failure Modes
Genuine breakout with retest (not a trap). Macro momentum overwhelms trap setup. Too early entry before close-back confirmed.

SECTION 4
Crypto Microstructure Scalper — L2 Signals, Funding, Cross-Exchange
4.1  Top-Book & Multi-Level Depth Imbalance
Edge
Top-book imbalance (bid qty vs ask qty at best level) predicts next-tick direction with 53–58% accuracy. Multi-level imbalance (top 5–10 levels) improves accuracy to 57–62% and extends predictive horizon to 5–30 seconds.
Formula/Rule
L1 Imbalance = (bid_qty[0] − ask_qty[0]) / (bid_qty[0] + ask_qty[0]). Range: −1 to +1. Multi-level: OBI_k = Σ(bid_qty[i] × w_i − ask_qty[i] × w_i) / Σ(bid_qty[i] + ask_qty[i]) for i=0..k. Weight decay: w_i = exp(−λ × i) where λ=0.5 typical. Trading rule: if OBI_5 >+0.3 → lean long next 10s. If OBI_5 <−0.3 → lean short. Combine with microprice: if OBI and microprice both bullish → double confidence.
Data Needed
L2 order book at ≤100ms granularity. Binance: free WebSocket L2 (top 20 levels). Bybit: same. Tardis.dev for historical. CoinAPI for normalized.
Best Markets
BTC-PERP/Binance (highest liquidity, tightest spread). ETH-PERP/Binance. SOL-PERP. Avoid small altcoins (spoofed books).
Holding Time
5–60 seconds.
Stop/Target
Stop = 1–2 ticks (≈0.01% on BTC). Target = 2–4 ticks or on OBI signal reversal.
Failure Modes
Spoofed orders inflate OBI (large order canceled before fill). OBI signal degrades during high vol (book refills faster than signal window). Stale snapshots >200ms.
Sources
Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (2014). Kolm, Tůmová & Turiel (2023) "Deep Order Book Imbalance". Tardis.dev microstructure research. Binance L2 WebSocket docs.
4.2  Microprice Bias
Edge
Microprice (volume-weighted mid between best bid and ask) is a better estimate of fair value than simple mid. Deviation of last trade price from microprice predicts reversion in next 5–15 seconds.
Formula/Rule
Microprice = (bid_px × ask_qty + ask_px × bid_qty) / (bid_qty + ask_qty). Bias = last_trade_price − microprice. If bias >+0.5 ticks → short lean (overbought vs fair value). If bias <−0.5 ticks → long lean. More sophisticated: weighted microprice using top 5 levels: μ* = Σ(P_i × Q_i × w_i) / Σ(Q_i × w_i).
Data Needed
L2 top of book + trade feed with timestamps. Sub-100ms granularity required for accuracy.
Best Markets
BTC-PERP, ETH-PERP, SOL-PERP on major perp exchanges.
Holding Time
5–20 seconds.
Stop/Target
1.5 ticks stop, 2.5 ticks target. Exit immediately if book refreshes against position.
Failure Modes
Large spread regimes make microprice less reliable. Thin book makes qty-weighted calculation noisy. High-volatility events cause price to gap past microprice.
Sources
Stoikov (2018) "The Micro-Price: A High-Frequency Estimator of Future Prices". Marco Avellaneda inventory model. Gould et al (2013) "Limit Order Books" survey.
4.3  Queue Depletion & Sweep Detection
Edge
When a large order sweeps through multiple price levels (queue depletion), it signals aggressive institutional directional intent. Next 1–5 minutes follow in sweep direction 62–68% of time.
Formula/Rule
Sweep detection: in window T=500ms, total trade volume >3× avg 500ms volume, covers ≥2 price levels, bid/ask spread temporarily widens after sweep. Queue depletion: best level size drops to 0 within 1 update interval. Sweep direction: if bid levels swept → aggressive selling → short lean. If ask levels swept → aggressive buying → long lean. Confirmation: next L2 snapshot shows slow book refill (not iceberg) after sweep.
Data Needed
L2 book updates at full depth (not just top-of-book), trade feed with aggressor flag. Tardis.dev for historical. Binance WebSocket for live.
Best Markets
BTC-PERP/Binance, ETH-PERP, CME Crypto futures.
Holding Time
30 seconds–5 minutes.
Stop/Target
Stop = opposite side of sweep level. Target = next major book cluster or 3× sweep range.
Failure Modes
Sweep followed by immediate reversal (large iceberg on other side). Flash crash sweeps have no follow-through (circuit breaker / bot response). Slow data pipeline missing sweep within detection window.
Sources
Kirilenko et al (2017) "The Flash Crash". CoinAPI crypto scalping guide. Bookmap sweep indicators. Tardis.dev Python client examples.
4.4  Absorption & Iceberg Heuristics
Edge
Absorption = large hidden order is absorbing aggressive flow without price moving. If price stays flat after large aggressor sell volume → strong hidden buyer (bullish). Opposite for seller absorption.
Formula/Rule
Absorption heuristic: trade volume in direction X increases >500% vs baseline, but price moves <0.05% in 30s → suspect absorption. Iceberg detection: order refreshes at same price level after each partial fill. If level size consistently replenishes → iceberg. Strength of absorption: volume_absorbed / expected_price_impact. If impact <0.1× expected → very strong absorption. Trade direction: if buy side absorbed → buy (institutional accumulation). If sell side absorbed → short (distribution).
Data Needed
L3 (market-by-order) data to see order-level events. Or infer from L2: if best bid level size drops then replenishes without a fill → iceberg signal. CoinAPI has L3 for some exchanges.
Best Markets
BTC-PERP on Binance and Bybit. ETH-PERP.
Holding Time
1–10 minutes.
Stop/Target
Stop = absorption level breaks (price finally moves through it). Target = 5–10× absorption zone to next major level.
Failure Modes
Absorption eventually fails (too much supply/demand). Fake absorption (coordinated manipulation). Low-liquidity periods where absorption is statistical noise.
Sources
Bookmap educational content on absorption. Jigsaw Trading order flow course. Aldridge "High-Frequency Trading" 2nd ed. Biais, Hillion, Spatt (1995) "An Empirical Analysis of the LOB".
4.5  Trade Aggressor Flow & Delta / 4.6  Spread Regime & Depth Freshness
Edge
Cumulative delta on crypto perps (taker buy − taker sell in window) is the most direct measure of directional pressure. Spread regime determines scalp viability: tight spread = scalp profitable, wide spread = avoid.
Formula/Rule
Crypto delta = Σ(buy_volume − sell_volume) per 1-min candle (available from exchange APIs). Signal: if 5-min delta >+2σ of rolling distribution → bullish. If <−2σ → bearish. Spread regime: classify as tight (<0.01% of price), normal (0.01–0.05%), wide (>0.05%). Only scalp in tight regime. Depth freshness: measure avg time for book to refill after trade. If refill time >2s → stale book → avoid scalping. Rule: bid-ask spread > (2 × target_profit) → do not enter.
Data Needed
Exchange trade feed with buy/sell classification (Binance marks aggressor side). L2 snapshots timed to track refill speed.
Best Markets
BTC/ETH perps on Binance, Bybit, OKX. Avoid CEX where spreads widen randomly (Kraken, Bitfinex during off-hours).
Holding Time
5–60 seconds (tight spread regime only).
Stop/Target
Stop = spread widens beyond threshold. Target = 2–3 ticks in tight regime.
Failure Modes
Spread spikes unexpectedly (news event). Delta signal works until HFT counters it. Binance fee change affects scalp viability (0.02% maker critical).
Sources
Binance/Bybit trade feed API docs. CryptoQuant delta data. Tardis.dev delta computation. Kaiko spread analytics.

4.8  Funding Rates, Open Interest & Liquidations
Edge
Extreme funding rates predict mean-reversion. High open interest with price at key level predicts breakout or liquidation cascade. Liquidation levels are publicly calculable and function as price magnets.

Liquidation cascade: identify liquidation price clusters using exchange liq heatmaps (CoinGlass). Price approaching large cluster = magnet (60–70% prob of reaching it). 4.9  Perp vs Spot Basis / 4.10  Cross-Exchange Lead-Lag
Edge
When perp trades at premium to spot (positive basis), leveraged longs are driving price and are vulnerable to deleveraging. Cross-exchange lead-lag: price discovery happens on the highest-volume exchange first, others follow within 100ms–2s.
Formula/Rule
Basis = (perp_price − spot_price) / spot_price × 100%. Normal range: 0 to +0.05%. Alert: >+0.15% = highly leveraged long → fade or avoid new longs. Lead-lag: Binance BTC spot → Coinbase BTC → Kraken BTC typically. Binance perp leads spot by 50–200ms. Cross-exchange arbitrage signal: if Binance perp +0.05% above Bybit perp → sell Binance, buy Bybit within 500ms. Price discovery leader: exchange with highest volume at trade initiation (use Kaiko cross-exchange trade feed to identify).
Data Needed
Perp + spot prices from same exchange (free via WebSocket). Cross-exchange lead-lag needs synchronized timestamps across exchanges (timestamp sync challenge — see Data section). Kaiko cross-exchange feed provides this.
Best Markets
BTC and ETH (most actively arb'd). Other large perps (SOL, BNB).
Holding Time
Basis trade: 1–8 hours (funding collection). Cross-exchange lag: 100ms–5 seconds.
Stop/Target
Basis: stop = basis widens further. Target = basis normalizes. Lead-lag: near-instant (automated).
Failure Modes
Basis stays elevated (sustained bullish sentiment). Cross-exchange arb requires HFT infrastructure (<10ms latency) to be consistently profitable. Exchange fee asymmetry kills arb margin.
Sources

4.11–4.14  Weekend/Weekday, Session Effects, Maker-Taker, Spoofing
Edge
Crypto shows consistent weekend seasonality (lower volume, wider spreads, larger impact of single large orders). Session effects mirror equity open/close patterns. Maker-taker fee structure fundamentally affects scalp viability.
Formula/Rule
Weekend effect: Saturday/Sunday vol is ~40% of weekday vol on BTC. Spread 1.5–2× wider. Reduce scalp size 50% or avoid. Session effects: Asia (00–08 UTC) = range-bound, funding-driven. Europe open (07–09 UTC) = vol expansion. US open (13:30–14:00 UTC) = highest vol. US close (20:00–21:00 UTC) = second vol peak. Maker-taker: Binance maker = −0.02% rebate, taker = +0.05%. Scalp requires limit orders (maker) to break even. If fill as taker → need >0.07% per round trip minimum target. Spoofing detection: large order appears, price moves toward it, order cancels before fill → spoof. Heuristic: if order >5× avg level size, track. If canceled within 500ms without fill → flag as spoof. Reduce OFI signal weight if spoof frequency >10% of updates.

Holding Time
Session context applied to all scalp decisions (filter layer, not standalone signal).
Stop/Target
Widen stops on weekends. In Asian session, fade extremes. In US session, follow momentum.
Failure Modes
Treating crypto as 24/7 uniform market. Ignoring maker-taker on margin (scalp math breaks with taker fees). Spoof detection creating false positives during genuine large orders.
Sources

SECTION 5
Execution / Live Trading — Fills, Smart Order Logic, Slippage
5.1  Real Broker/Exchange Fill Streams & Partial Fills
Edge
Understanding fill events in real-time allows immediate risk management, PnL calculation, and position reconciliation. Unhandled partial fills are a primary source of unintended exposure.
Formula/Rule
Fill stream components: fill_id, order_id, symbol, side, qty_filled, price, timestamp, remaining_qty, status (partial/full/canceled). Partial fill handling: track remaining_qty per order. If partial fill → re-evaluate whether to cancel/replace or wait. Fill quality score = |actual_fill_price − expected_price| / spread. Score <0.5 = good. >1.5 = investigate. Reconciliation: every 5 min → compare broker position feed with internal tracker. Alert if divergence >0 shares/contracts.
Data Needed
Broker WebSocket execution report feed (FIX 4.2+ or REST). For US equities: IBKR TWS API, Alpaca, TD Ameritrade. For crypto: Binance order status WebSocket, Bybit executionReport.
Best Markets
Universal — applies to all three lanes.
Holding Time
N/A — real-time event stream.
Stop/Target
Cancel open orders immediately if: position reconciliation fails, or market moves >3× stop before fill completes.
Failure Modes
WebSocket disconnection mid-order leaving zombie order. Race condition between cancel and fill. Broker-side partial fill not reported in real-time. Duplicate fill messages.
Sources
IBKR TWS API docs. Alpaca execution docs. Binance WebSocket streams documentation. FIX protocol guide (fixtrading.org). Schwager "Market Wizards" (execution discipline).
5.2  Smart Order Placement — Limit vs Market Decision Rules
Edge
Systematic limit-vs-market decision eliminates the worst fills (chasing market orders) while ensuring fills when momentum demands speed.
Formula/Rule
Use limit orders when: (1) signal strength <+2σ, (2) spread >0.05%, (3) holding time >5 min. Use market orders when: (1) momentum signal >+3σ urgency, (2) stop-loss exit, (3) end-of-day forced flat, (4) risk management kill switch. Limit placement rule: join best bid/ask for passive entry. If not filled in N seconds, slide 1 tick toward mid. If still unfilled at T=2N → cancel or convert to market. Iceberg order: expose only 10–20% of intended size at a time to avoid front-running. Crypto: post-only orders → always limit (guarantees maker rebate on Binance).
Data Needed
Real-time spread, current OFI/signal strength meter, time-to-fill tracker.
Best Markets
Universal. Post-only critical for crypto scalping. Market orders acceptable for ES/NQ stop outs (tight spread).
Holding Time
N/A — order type decision per trade.
Stop/Target
Limit: can miss fill in fast market (accept this). Market: guaranteed fill but worst price. Hybrid: limit with T-second conversion to market.
Failure Modes
Limit in fast market → missed fill, full loss of setup edge. Market order in wide-spread market → major slippage. Iceberg discovered by HFT → front-run.
Sources
Kissell "The Science of Algorithmic Trading". Binance post-only order type docs. IEX exchange maker-taker rules. IBKR adaptive algorithm documentation.
5.3  Cancel/Replace Logic & Fill Quality Measurement
Edge
Systematic cancel/replace reduces average fill cost by 0.5–2 bps versus static limit orders. Fill quality measurement identifies execution degradation before it compounds.
Formula/Rule
Cancel/replace trigger: if OFI reverses >50% from entry signal within fill window → cancel order. Replace strategy: re-evaluate signal. If still valid → replace 1 tick more aggressive. If invalid → abort. Fill quality metrics: (1) Arrival cost = fill_price − arrival_mid. Negative = good (filled below mid). (2) VWAP shortfall = fill_price − interval_VWAP. (3) Implementation shortfall = (fill_price − decision_price) × qty. Alert if: avg arrival cost >+1.5 bps across 50 trades. Investigate broker routing.
Data Needed
Fill timestamps matched to bid/ask at fill time, VWAP at fill time, decision price (signal time quote).
Best Markets
US equities, ES/NQ futures, crypto perps.
Holding Time
N/A — execution quality tracking.
Stop/Target
Reject setups where historical fill quality shows persistent >2 bps shortfall (broker/venue problem).
Failure Modes
Cancel in fast market causes missed fill. Aggressive replace escalates market impact. Fill quality calc skewed by volatility at fill time (use spread-normalized metrics instead).
Sources
Almgren-Chriss execution model. IEX investor education (iexcloud.io). Kissell "Science of Algorithmic Trading". JP Morgan execution quality research.
5.4  Latency Decay of Setups & Slippage Models by Symbol/Regime
Edge
Every edge has a latency decay function — the longer between signal and execution, the less edge remains. Knowing this decay prevents acting on stale signals.
Formula/Rule
Decay model: edge(t) = edge(0) × exp(−λ × t) where t = seconds since signal. Estimate λ from your data: regress fill-time-adjusted PnL on execution lag. Typical λ for OFI scalp: 0.15/sec. For swing setup: 0.02/day. Decision: if E[edge(t_execution)] < E[slippage] → do not trade. Slippage model by symbol: small_cap_slippage = 0.5% per $100K. Large_cap = 0.05% per $100K. ES futures = 0.5 tick = 0.005% per $5M. BTC perp = 0.02% per $1M. Regime multiplier: slippage × 2 during VIX >30. Slippage × 3 during open 30 min.
Data Needed
Trade log with signal time, order sent time, fill time. PnL tagged to each time step for regression.
Best Markets
Decay fastest in scalping (5–30s decay). Slowest in swing (24–72h decay). Model per lane separately.
Holding Time
N/A — pre-trade decision model.
Stop/Target
Hard rule: if execution lag > 2× decay half-life → abort entry.
Failure Modes
Not measuring signal-to-execution latency. Using static slippage assumption regardless of vol regime. HFT reacting to your order before fill.
Sources
Almgren & Chriss (2000). Grinold & Kahn "Active Portfolio Management" (2nd ed). Avellaneda & Stoikov market-making model. Your own execution log regression.

6.3  Timestamp Synchronization / 6.4  WebSocket Reliability & Failover
Edge
Unsynchronized timestamps make cross-exchange analysis meaningless and create false lead-lag signals. WebSocket drops during high-volatility events cause missed executions and open risk.
Formula/Rule

Latency = local_receipt − exchange_send. Alert if >200ms. WebSocket reliability: implement ping/pong heartbeat every 20s. If no pong within 30s → reconnect. Failover: maintain 2 parallel WebSocket connections to same exchange from different IPs. On primary disconnect → switch to backup within 50ms. Snapshot reset: on WebSocket reconnect → request fresh L2 snapshot immediately before processing incremental updates. Crypto exchanges: Binance recommends reconnecting after 24h regardless of health (avoid stale connections).
Data Needed

Best Markets

Holding Time
N/A — infrastructure.
Stop/Target
Max acceptable timestamp drift: <5ms for scalping, <100ms for day trading, <500ms for swing.
Failure Modes
Single WebSocket connection dropping at critical moment. Using system clock without NTP sync (drift accumulates over hours). Not handling exchange maintenance windows (planned disconnect).

Risk & Portfolio Management — Sizing, Kill Switches, Drawdown Controls
7.1  Strategy-Level Capital Allocation
Edge
Allocating capital to strategies in proportion to their risk-adjusted returns (not just returns) maximizes Sharpe of the overall system.
Formula/Rule
Starting allocation: Swing 40% / Day Trading 35% / Crypto Scalper 25%. Risk parity rebalance: w_i = (1/σ_i) / Σ(1/σ_j) where σ_i = 20-day realized vol of lane i. Rebalance trigger: if any lane's risk contribution deviates >5% from target → rebalance. Min allocation: 15% per lane (never go below — kills diversification benefit). Max: 55% per lane. Capital efficiency: ensure unused capital in one lane doesn't sit idle — keep in money market or T-bills.
Data Needed
Daily returns per lane, rolling 20-day vol per lane, correlation matrix.
Best Markets
Universal — meta-framework across all three lanes.
Holding Time
Allocation reviewed weekly, rebalanced monthly or on trigger.
Stop/Target
If any lane hits −10% month → mandatory review before re-deploying capital to that lane.
Failure Modes
Putting 80% into hot lane after good month. Not accounting for correlation between swing and day trading (both are US equities).
Sources
Ilmanen "Expected Returns". AQR risk parity white papers. Qian (2005) "Risk Parity Portfolios". Dalio Bridgewater All Weather methodology.
7.2  Max Daily Loss, Consecutive-Loss Shutoffs & Drawdown Controls
Edge
Systematic loss limits prevent catastrophic blowups. Human traders in drawdown make worse decisions (tilt). Automated limits enforce discipline when judgment is impaired.
Formula/Rule
Daily max loss: hard stop at −2% of account per day. Soft warning at −1.5% (reduce size). Consecutive loss shutoff: if 3 consecutive full-size losers → reduce to 50% size. If 5 consecutive → halt trading for rest of session. Drawdown controls: Drawdown <5% from equity peak: normal operations. Drawdown 5–10%: reduce all positions by 50%. Drawdown 10–15%: halt new entries, close existing if against position. Drawdown >15%: full halt, manual review required. Formula: drawdown = (peak_equity − current_equity) / peak_equity.
Data Needed
Real-time PnL tracker, equity peak tracker, consecutive loss counter, position monitor.
Best Markets
Universal. Critical for crypto scalper (volatility regime can rapidly accelerate drawdown).
Holding Time
Kill switch triggers are permanent for that session unless manually reset.
Stop/Target
These are the stops for the overall system — not per-trade stops.
Failure Modes
Not automating the shutoff (human override during tilt). Applying lane-level shutoff to whole account incorrectly. Not distinguishing between bad luck and bad process.
Sources
Schwager "Market Wizards" (consistent theme). Sperandeo "Trader Vic". Trading firm risk management frameworks (Jane Street, Citadel documented principles). Kahneman "Thinking Fast and Slow" (tilt psychology).
7.3  Correlation Clustering & Exposure Limits
Edge
During market stress, correlations spike toward 1.0 (diversification evaporates). Pre-identifying correlated clusters allows reducing exposure before the cluster activates.
Formula/Rule
Correlation matrix: compute rolling 20-day pairwise correlation across all open positions. Cluster detection: hierarchical clustering on correlation matrix (scipy.cluster.hierarchy). Group positions where corr >0.6. Cluster exposure limit: no more than 25% of capital in one cluster. Symbol cap: no more than 5% of capital in any single name. Sector cap: no more than 25% of equity in one GICS sector. Venue cap: no more than 40% of total capital on one exchange (counterparty risk). Vol-adjusted exposure: net_exposure = Σ(position_value × β_to_SPY). Keep net β <1.5.
Data Needed
Real-time position values, daily returns history, beta estimates, GICS sector classifications.
Best Markets
Universal. Crypto: Binance concentration risk — cap at 40% of crypto capital.
Holding Time
Monitor continuously intraday.
Stop/Target
Alert if cluster exposure >20%. Hard stop at >25%. Reduce immediately.
Failure Modes
Assuming low correlation persists in stress events. Treating ETF + components as uncorrelated. Missing hidden correlation between swing and day trading positions.
Sources
Meucci "Risk and Asset Allocation". Lopez de Prado "Machine Learning for Asset Managers" (Ch 6 clustering). Ledoit & Wolf (2004) "Honey I Shrunk the Sample Covariance Matrix".
7.4  Volatility-Adjusted Sizing, Risk-On/Risk-Off Mode & Kill Switches
Edge
Position size inversely proportional to realized volatility maintains constant dollar risk per trade regardless of market conditions. Risk-on/off mode shifts capital between high-beta and defensive positions.
Formula/Rule
Vol-adjusted sizing: shares = (account × risk_pct) / (price × σ_normalized × ATR_multiplier). σ_normalized = (current_ATR / 20-day_avg_ATR). If σ_normalized >2 → halve size. Risk-on mode (default): full allocation, momentum strategies active, beta >1 acceptable. Risk-off trigger: VIX >25, or SPY drops >2% in 1 session, or crypto realized vol >80% annualized. Risk-off actions: reduce equity long exposure by 50%, close crypto positions >5x leverage, shift swing capital to T-bills or short-duration bonds. Kill switch triggers: (1) Abnormal slippage: fills consistently >3× model → halt, investigate venue. (2) Bad data: bid >ask for >10 consecutive updates → halt, check feed. (3) Position reconciliation fail: broker pos ≠ internal pos → halt, reconcile. (4) Drawdown kill: as per Section 7.2.
Data Needed
Real-time VIX feed, current ATR, realized vol calculation, position reconciliation system, slippage monitor.
Best Markets
Universal. Kill switches critical for crypto scalper and live day trading.
Holding Time
Risk-off mode: stay until trigger clears for 3+ consecutive sessions.
Stop/Target
Kill switch is a system-level stop. No new trades until manually cleared after investigation.
Failure Modes
Not automating kill switches. Vol-adjusted sizing not recalculated frequently enough (stale ATR). Risk-off mode not covering crypto when equity vol spikes (crypto follows equities in risk-off).
Sources
AQR "Volatility Managed Portfolios" (Moreira & Muir 2017). CME volatility threshold documentation. CBOE VIX methodology. Pardo "Evaluation & Optimization" (system-level stops).
